\section{MCMC and 2D Ising Model [30 Points]}
\materials{Lectures 14--17, Lab 11}

Consider the 2D Ising model in a square lattice $\Lambda$ with $20\times 20$ sites and periodic boundary conditions.
The energy of the system for a given spin configuration $\sigma = \{\sigma_i\}_{i\in \Lambda}$ is
\begin{equation}
  E(\sigma) = -J\sum_{\langle ij\rangle} \sigma_i\sigma_j,
\end{equation}
where $\langle i j \rangle$ denotes two adjacent sites (with no double counting), $J$ is the spin-spin interaction, and $\sigma_i \in \{ -1, +1\}$ is the spin at site $i$.

The net magnetization of the system is
\begin{equation}
  M(\sigma) = \frac{1}{|\Lambda|}\sum_{i\in \Lambda} \sigma_i.
\end{equation}

The probability of a given configuration $\sigma$ is given by the Boltzmann distribution
\begin{equation}
  p(\sigma) = \frac{1}{Z}\exp \left(-\frac{E(\sigma)}{k_\mathrm{B}T}\right),
\end{equation}
where $Z = \sum_{\sigma} \exp \left(-\frac{E(\sigma)}{k_\mathrm{B}T}\right)$ is the partition function, $T$ is the temperature, and $k_\mathrm{B}$ is the Boltzmann constant.
For this problem, you may set $J=k_\mathrm{B}=1$.

\section{Path-Integral Monte Carlo at Finite Temperature [20 Points]}
\materials{Lectures 13 and 16--19, Labs 13--14}

In Lab 14 you sampled imaginary-time paths at large $\beta$, where only the ground state contributes.
Here you will work at moderate $\beta$, where excited states contribute too, and check the result against an exact answer and against the eigenvalue method of Lecture 11 and Assignment 2.

Divide the imaginary time $\beta$ into $N$ slices of width $\delta\tau = \beta/N$ (with $m = \hbar = 1$).
A closed path $x = (x_0, x_1, \ldots, x_{N-1})$, with $x_N \equiv x_0$, has probability
\begin{equation}
  p(x) \propto e^{-S_E(x)}, \qquad
  S_E(x) = \sum_{i=0}^{N-1}\left[\frac{(x_{i+1}-x_i)^2}{2\,\delta\tau} + \delta\tau\, V(x_i)\right],
\end{equation}
and the thermal average of $x^2$ is the average of $\frac{1}{N}\sum_i x_i^2$ over sampled paths.
For the harmonic oscillator, $V(x) = \frac{1}{2}x^2$, the exact result as $\delta\tau \to 0$ is
\begin{equation}
  \langle x^2\rangle_\beta = \frac{1}{2}\coth\left(\frac{\beta}{2}\right).
\end{equation}
