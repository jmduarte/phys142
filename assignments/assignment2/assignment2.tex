\section{Tunneling in the Double Potential Well [40 Points + 10 Bonus Points]}
\materials{Lectures 10--11}

Consider the double potential well,
\begin{equation}
  V(x) = \alpha x^4 - 2x^2 + \frac{1}{\alpha},
\end{equation}
where $x$ is the position of the particle, and we set $m=\hbar=1$.
The minima of the potential are at $x = \pm x_\mathrm{min}$, with $x_\mathrm{min} = 1/\sqrt{\alpha}$ and $V(\pm x_\mathrm{min}) = 0$, and the barrier height is $V(0) = 1/\alpha$.
The potential is shown below for $\alpha = 0.4$.
\begin{center}
  \includegraphics[width=0.5\textwidth]{V_all.pdf}
\end{center}

Your value of $\alpha$ is set by your Triton Student Number (TSN: nine digits starting with 2),
\begin{equation}
  \alpha = 0.3 + \frac{d}{5000},
\end{equation}
where $d$ is the last three digits of your TSN (000 to 999), so that $0.3 \leq \alpha < 0.5$.
For example, a TSN of 212345678 gives $\alpha = 0.3 + 678/5000 = 0.4356$, and 212345123 gives $\alpha = 0.3246$.
State your $\alpha$ at the top of your report, and use it everywhere except where a problem gives other values.

Near each minimum the potential is approximately harmonic, $V''(\pm x_\mathrm{min}) = 8$ for every $\alpha$,
\begin{align}
  V_+(x) & = 4(x-x_\mathrm{min})^2, \\
  V_-(x) & = 4(x+x_\mathrm{min})^2,
\end{align}
so $\omega = 2\sqrt{2}$.
The ground states of these approximate wells are
\begin{equation}
  \psi_\pm(x) = \left(\frac{\omega}{\pi}\right)^{1/4} \exp\left(- \frac{\omega}{2}(x\mp x_\mathrm{min})^2\right),
\end{equation}
and the symmetric and antisymmetric combinations
\begin{equation}
  \psi_\mathrm{S}(x) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left( \psi_+(x) + \psi_-(x) \right), \qquad
  \psi_\mathrm{A}(x) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left( \psi_+(x) - \psi_-(x) \right)
\end{equation}
approximate the ground state $\phi_0$ and the first excited state $\phi_1$.
The energy gap between them is $\Delta E = E_1 - E_0$.
We estimate the tunneling time $t_\mathrm{tunnel}$ as the time of the first major minimum of $\langle x\rangle(t)$ in the slow oscillation from the right well to the left well.
This is the first maximum leftward displacement of the slow oscillation, not the first crossing of $\langle x\rangle=0$ or a small local minimum caused by faster oscillations.

Use $N_D + 1$ grid points $x_0 = -4, x_1, \ldots, x_{N_D} = +4$ with $N_D = 600$ (so $\Delta x = 8/600$), and time step $\epsilon = \Delta t = \pi/128$.
Use this grid throughout except for the convergence checks in Problem B(ii); all results saved to \texttt{results.json} must use the original grid.
Approximate spatial integrals by $\Delta x\sum_{i=0}^{N_D}$, including both endpoints, and use this convention for propagation, normalization, expectation values, and overlaps.

\begin{problem}[10]
  Demonstrate tunneling with the Feynman path integral.
  Start with the particle in the right well, $\psi(x, 0) = \psi_+(x)$, and evolve the wave function one time step at a time with the elementary propagator
  \begin{equation}
    \tilde{\mathcal K}(x_b, \epsilon; x_a, 0) = \exp \left[ i \left(\frac{1}{2}\frac{(x_b - x_a)^2}{\epsilon} - V\left(\frac{x_a+x_b}{2}\right) \epsilon\right) \right],
  \end{equation}
  where $x_a$ and $x_b$ are the initial and final positions.
  Use exactly this form, with the potential evaluated at the midpoint, so that your results can be autograded.
  This approximation lacks the normalization factor, so normalize the wave function after every step,
  \begin{equation}
    \psi(x, t) \leftarrow \frac{\psi(x, t)}{\sqrt{\int |\psi(x, t)|^2\, dx}}.

\end{problem}
